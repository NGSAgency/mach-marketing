Most of the cooling calls we take in Olathe come from homes built between the mid-nineties and the mid-2000s, which puts their original condensers at the end of a normal service life. A unit of that age rarely fails structurally. It fails at the capacitor, the contactor or the fan motor, and those are repairs rather than replacements.

That matters for what a repair visit here looks like. We arrive expecting a component fault and carrying the parts for it, so the majority of Olathe repair calls are finished on the first visit rather than booked back in for a part.

The newer streets south of 151st are a different job. Two-stage systems there fail at the control board or a sensor, which needs the diagnostic equipment rather than a spare part on the van, and takes longer to find and less time to fix.

Older houses closer to downtown Olathe bring a third pattern: the system is fine and the ductwork is not. We check static pressure and airflow before condemning a compressor, because replacing a working unit does not fix a house that cannot move the air.

Through a Johnson County July the compressors run close to continuously, and the first sustained week above 95°F produces most of our summer call volume. If your unit is struggling in May it is worth having it looked at before that week rather than during it.
