What it covers

What the job involves

Most calls come down to one of four things: a refrigerant leak, a failed capacitor or contactor, a blocked condensate line, or a system that was never sized right for the house. We test for all of them before quoting, because replacing a part that was only a symptom is how people end up paying twice.

We work on every residential brand sold in this market, including systems we did not install, and we carry the common failure parts on the van so most repairs finish on the first visit.

Know the signs

Signs you need AC repair

What people notice first, in the words they use when they call.

  • The air coming out of the vents is warm

    The system is running but it is no longer cooling. Usually low refrigerant from a leak, or a failed compressor. It will not fix itself, and running it in this state can make the repair bigger.

  • It runs all day and the house never gets cool

    A unit that never reaches the thermostat setting is working far harder than it should. Sometimes it is only a clogged filter or a dirty outdoor coil. Sometimes the system is undersized or losing refrigerant.

  • There is water pooling around the indoor unit

    The drain line is blocked and the condensate has nowhere to go. A cheap fix caught early and a ceiling repair caught late, so it is worth a call the same week.

  • You hear grinding, squealing or banging when it starts

    Noise that was not there last summer means a part is worn or loose. A bearing or a blower wheel caught early is a small job. Left alone it usually takes the motor with it.

  • Your electric bill jumped and nothing else changed

    A system losing efficiency runs longer to do the same work. A steadily climbing bill through the cooling season is often the first sign of a problem you cannot hear yet.
