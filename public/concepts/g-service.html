What the job involves

Most calls come down to one of four things: a refrigerant leak, a failed capacitor or contactor, a blocked condensate line, or a system that was never sized right for the house.

We test for all four before quoting, because replacing a part that was only a symptom is how people end up paying twice. On a leak we find where it is going rather than topping it up and booking you in again next summer.

We work on every residential brand sold in this market, including systems we did not install, and the van carries the common failure parts, so most repairs finish on the first visit.

Age changes the answer. Under about ten years a repair is almost always worth doing. Between ten and fifteen it depends on what failed and what the system cost to run last summer. Past fifteen, a compressor or a coil is usually money spent twice, and we will say so rather than sell you the repair.

If the house has never cooled evenly, that is a different conversation from a breakdown. Uneven cooling is usually ductwork, and no replacement fixes it. We measure before recommending anything, because the cheapest honest answer is what brings people back.

Know the signs

Signs you need AC repair

What people notice first, in the words they use when they call.

  • The air coming out of the vents is warm

    The system is running but no longer cooling. Usually low refrigerant from a leak, or a failed compressor. Running it in this state makes the repair bigger.

  • It runs all day and the house never gets cool

    A unit that never reaches the setting is working far harder than it should. Sometimes a clogged filter or a dirty coil. Sometimes it was undersized from the start.

  • There is water pooling around the indoor unit

    The drain line is blocked and the condensate has nowhere to go. A cheap fix caught early and a ceiling repair caught late, so it is worth a call the same week.

  • You hear grinding, squealing or banging

    Noise that was not there last summer means a part is worn or loose. A bearing caught early is a small job; left alone it usually takes the motor with it.

  • Your electric bill jumped and nothing changed

    A system losing efficiency runs longer to do the same work. A climbing bill through the cooling season is often the first sign of a problem you cannot hear.

  • It turns on and off every few minutes

    Short cycling wears the compressor out faster than anything else. Usually a sensor, a dirty coil or refrigerant, and worth diagnosing before the compressor fails.

Yes. We service every residential brand sold in this market.

Should I repair or replace?

We tell you the cost of both and what the system has left in it. Past about fifteen years, a big repair is usually money spent twice.
