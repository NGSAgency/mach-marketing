03About Olathe

What we see in Olathe homes

20–30 Years is the typical age of the systems we are called to in Olathe — the window in which the original unit reaches the end of its life.

Olathe grew quickly through the 1990s and 2000s, so a large share of the homes we visit are between twenty and thirty years old. That is exactly the window in which the original air conditioning system reaches the end of its service life, and it is why replacement quotes are a bigger share of our Olathe work than of our work further east.

The newer subdivisions south of 151st have tighter building envelopes and two-stage systems, which fail differently: fewer refrigerant problems, more control board and sensor faults. The older streets closer to downtown Olathe still have plenty of single-stage units and original ductwork, where airflow is as often the cause of a warm house as the compressor is.

Summer humidity in Johnson County puts a long, hard run on a compressor from June through August. A unit that was coping in May will often fail in the first sustained week above 95°F, which is why we hold more capacitors and contactors on the vans through the summer than any other part.

Winter is the mirror of it. The first hard freeze brings furnace calls from homes where the system ran all last winter without trouble — most of them ignition or flame sensor faults rather than anything structural.
