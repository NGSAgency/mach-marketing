AC repair in this part of town

The call we get most in Overland Park is a house that cools the front rooms and never quite reaches the back.

In the 1970s stock north of 95th that is almost always the return ducting rather than the condenser, and a bigger unit makes it worse rather than better. We measure static pressure before quoting anything, which now and then turns a replacement quote into a much cheaper piece of duct work.

South of 151st the usual call is short cycling in a two-storey build on a single-stage system. That is a sizing problem, not a fault, and it is worth knowing before you pay to repair a compressor that is going to fail again.

The humidity here decides more than the temperature does. A system that cools the air quickly and shuts off leaves an Overland Park house feeling damp in July, and people read that as the unit being tired when it is actually running too short. It changes what we recommend and it is the reason we ask how the house feels rather than only what the thermostat says.

Replacements here are inspected by the city. We pull the permit and book the inspection, which adds about a week to the paperwork and nothing at all to your day.
